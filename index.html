Introduction to Cybersecurity Beginner

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting systems, networks, and programs from digital attacks. These attacks are usually aimed at accessing, changing, or destroying sensitive information or extorting money.

  • Understand the CIA Triad: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability
  • Learn common threat actors: hackers, insider threats, nation-states
  • Recognize the difference between vulnerabilities and exploits
  • Explore basic threat categories: malware, phishing, MITM, DoS
2
Password Security Best Practices Beginner

Weak passwords are one of the most common causes of security breaches. A strong password strategy is your first line of defense.

  • Use at least 12 characters — more is better
  • Mix uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and special characters
  • Never reuse passwords across different accounts
  • Use a reputable password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password)
  • Enable multi-factor authentication (MFA) everywhere
3
Recognizing Phishing Attacks Intermediate

Phishing is a technique used to steal credentials or install malware by disguising malicious content as a legitimate source.

  • Check sender addresses carefully for lookalike domains
  • Hover over links before clicking — confirm the destination
  • Be suspicious of urgent language ("Act now!", "Your account is suspended")
  • Look for poor grammar, generic greetings ("Dear Customer")
  • Verify requests for sensitive info via a separate channel
4
Malware & Ransomware Awareness Intermediate

Malware is malicious software designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to a system. Ransomware is a type that encrypts data and demands payment.

  • Never open email attachments from unknown senders
  • Keep your OS and all software updated with security patches
  • Use reputable antivirus / endpoint protection software
  • Maintain regular, offline backups of critical data
  • Disable macros in Office documents from the internet
5
Network Security Fundamentals Intermediate

Securing your network prevents unauthorized access and eavesdropping on your data in transit.

  • Use WPA3 or WPA2 encryption on your Wi-Fi networks
  • Change default router credentials immediately after setup
  • Enable a firewall on your router and all devices
  • Separate guest and corporate/personal traffic with VLANs
  • Use a VPN on public Wi-Fi to encrypt your traffic
6
Social Engineering & Human Hacking Advanced

Social engineering exploits human psychology rather than technical vulnerabilities. Attackers manipulate trust, fear, and urgency to bypass security controls.

  • Pretexting: attacker creates a fabricated scenario to extract info
  • Baiting: leaving infected USB drives in parking lots
  • Vishing: phone-based social engineering impersonating IT support
  • Always verify identity before granting access or sharing data
  • Report suspicious contact attempts to your security team

🔒 HTTPS & Encryption

HTTPS (HyperText Transfer Protocol Secure) encrypts the connection between your browser and the server, preventing eavesdropping and tampering.

Always look for the padlock icon in your browser's address bar. However, note that HTTPS only encrypts the connection — a phishing site can also have HTTPS, so check the domain too.

✓ DO

  • Check for padlock before entering data
  • Use browser extensions like HTTPS Everywhere
  • Look for EV certificates on banking sites
  • Verify the exact domain name

✗ DON'T

  • Enter credentials on HTTP sites
  • Ignore certificate warnings
  • Assume HTTPS = trustworthy domain
  • Accept self-signed certificates blindly

🔍 HTTPS vs HTTP — at a glance

SAFE
https://www.yourbank.com/login
Encrypted connection, correct domain
RISK
http://www.yourbank.com/login
No encryption — credentials sent in plaintext
WARN
https://yourbank.secure-login.net
HTTPS present but domain is suspicious — not yourbank.com
RISK
https://yourb4nk.com/login
Typosquatting — "4" instead of "a"

🔗 URL Inspection

Before clicking any link, inspect the full URL. Attackers use look-alike domains, long confusing paths, and URL shorteners to hide malicious destinations.

Hover over a link in your email client or browser to preview the real destination before clicking.

✓ DO

  • Hover links before clicking
  • Expand shortened URLs (e.g., unshorten.it)
  • Type known URLs directly in the address bar
  • Use browser bookmarks for important sites

✗ DON'T

  • Click shortened links blindly
  • Trust misleading anchor text
  • Ignore redirect warnings
  • Open links from unsolicited emails

🔍 URL Red Flags

SAFE
https://paypal.com/myaccount
Correct domain, clean path
RISK
https://paypal.com.secure-update.xyz/login
Real domain appears in subdomain — actual domain is .xyz
WARN
https://bit.ly/3xK9pQ
Shortened URL — destination unknown without expanding
RISK
https://paypa1.com/login
Lookalike domain — "1" instead of "l"

🍪 Cookies & Privacy

Cookies are small files stored by websites to remember your session and preferences. Third-party tracking cookies, however, follow you across the web and build detailed profiles.

Understanding cookies helps you make informed decisions about what data you share and with whom.

✓ DO

  • Review cookie settings on websites
  • Reject non-essential cookies when possible
  • Clear cookies regularly
  • Use privacy-focused browsers (Firefox, Brave)

✗ DON'T

  • Accept all cookies without reading
  • Stay logged in to unneeded accounts
  • Ignore tracking cookie notices
  • Use the same browser profile for everything

🍪 Cookie Types Explained

OK
Session cookies
Temporary; deleted when browser closes. Used for login state.
WARN
Persistent cookies
Survive after session. Used for "remember me" — check expiry.
RISK
Third-party tracking cookies
Set by ad networks — track you across many sites.
OK
HttpOnly + Secure cookies
Best practice — protected from JavaScript and sent only over HTTPS.

📶 Public Wi-Fi Safety

Public Wi-Fi networks (coffee shops, airports, hotels) are often unencrypted or poorly secured. Attackers can run "Evil Twin" hotspots or perform man-in-the-middle attacks to intercept your data.

Never trust a network just because it has a familiar name — verify with staff before connecting.

✓ DO

  • Use a VPN whenever on public Wi-Fi
  • Verify the network name with staff
  • Stick to HTTPS-only sites
  • Turn off Wi-Fi when not in use

✗ DON'T

  • Log into banking on public Wi-Fi (without VPN)
  • Auto-connect to open networks
  • Share sensitive files on public networks
  • Assume "free Wi-Fi" is safe Wi-Fi

📶 Attack Scenarios

RISK
Evil Twin Attack
Attacker clones a legitimate network name to intercept traffic
RISK
Man-in-the-Middle (MITM)
Attacker silently relays and alters communication between you and sites
WARN
Packet Sniffing
Unencrypted HTTP traffic readable by anyone on the same network
FIX
VPN (Virtual Private Network)
Encrypts all your traffic — mitigates MITM and sniffing risks

📱 Social Media Safety

Oversharing on social media can enable targeted attacks. Attackers gather publicly available information (OSINT) to craft convincing spear-phishing messages or guess security questions.

Review your privacy settings regularly and think before you post — even seemingly harmless details can be pieced together.

✓ DO

  • Set profiles to private / friends-only
  • Use unique usernames per platform
  • Enable login notifications & MFA
  • Review connected third-party apps

✗ DON'T

  • Share your location in real-time
  • Post photos of ID/financial documents
  • Reveal answers to security questions
  • Click on polls asking for personal data

🕵️ OSINT Red Flags

RISK
Birthday & hometown public
Often used as security question answers — hide from public view
RISK
Vacation posts (real-time)
Announces you're away from home — burglary risk
WARN
Job title & employer visible
Enables targeted spear-phishing pretending to be your employer
TIP
Regular privacy audit
Review what's public every 3 months — platforms change defaults
